# Positioning Angles: App Builder

Date: 2026-11-10. Skill: `positioning-angles`. Reads [idea.md](idea.md), [validation.md](validation.md), [differentiators.md](differentiators.md).

Audience (from idea.md): indie founders who build subscription apps with AI and want them live and earning on the App Store.

## Step 1: What they are really buying

Not "an app." The moment after "Submit for Review" when nothing comes back rejected, the paywall works, and the listing shows up in search. The pain that goes away: weeks of rejection loops ("3 months and 10+ rejections") and the fear of a spam flag on the developer account.

## Step 2: Alternatives and their weak spots

| Alternative | Weak spot |
|---|---|
| Do nothing (submit and hope) | Rejections arrive one at a time, days apart |
| DIY with Expo Launch / EAS | Free submission, but no checks, no paywall, no listing |
| Rork / Vibecode / Superapp / Newly | All say "publish". Rork's reviewer is paid and skips 4.3. Quality complaints past a few screens. Credits burn on fixes. |
| Upwork freelancer | Costs per job, slow to hire, quality varies (budgets unverified) |
| Separate ASO / screenshot tools | One more tool and one more bill. Not tied to the app's real screens. |

## Step 3: Mechanism

**Pre-flight before Apple:** checks mapped to named guidelines (2.1, 3.1.1/3.1.2, 4.2, 4.3, 4.8, 5.1.1) plus a **distinct-by-design** check against your own other apps. The listing kit (screenshots and keywords) is made from the app's real screens.

Status: listing kit and paywall are **live**. Pre-flight and the 4.3 check are **planned** ([differentiators.md](differentiators.md)).

## Step 4: Market stage

**Stage 3 (crowded).** Nineteen-plus AI mobile builders. All claim "native" and "publish." Skepticism is high ("Rork is not worth the hype", "nobody uses your vibecoded apps"). Stage 3 calls for the **mechanism** angle: explain *why* this gets through when others don't. There are hints of Stage 4 identity ("app founders", Superapp). That makes an identity angle a later option.

## Angle options

**Angle 1: Check before Apple does (mechanism + enemy). LEAD**
- The angle: App Builder runs Apple's review rules and a look-alike check on your app before you submit, so rejections and spam flags are caught on your screen, not in App Review.
- Why it works: the number-one complaint in the category is rejection. Apple's June 2026 4.3 change made the fear concrete: account removal for repeat look-alikes. A mechanism tied to named guideline numbers is believable to a skeptical Stage 3 buyer.
- Headline directions:
  - "Check your app against Apple's rules before Apple does."
  - "Apple can now remove developers who keep submitting look-alike apps. Check yours before you submit."
  - "Your app works. Now get it through review the first time." (Use "first time" only as an aim, never as a guarantee.)
- When to use: after pre-flight and the 4.3 check ship. Until then it is a waitlist angle ("coming: …"), not a live claim.

**Angle 2: The free launch kit for any app (speed/ease + specificity). LIVE NOW**
- The angle: free App Store screenshots and keywords made from your app's real screens, whichever tool built it.
- Why it works: "app store screenshot generator" is up 69%, and the SERP is weak. It asks for nothing (no rebuild, no credits) and shows quality straight away.
- Headline directions:
  - "Free App Store screenshots and keywords. For any app, built with any tool."
  - "Your listing in 10 minutes, made from your real screens." (Check the time on real runs before publishing a number.)
- When to use: top of funnel, SEO pages, community posts. True today.

**Angle 3: Built by someone who ships a subscription app (social proof, honest version)**
- The angle: the paywall, onboarding and listing templates are the ones Tayo uses on Weight Class, a live calorie and GLP-1 tracker.
- Why it works: makers distrust tool vendors who have never shipped. A live app anyone can download is checkable proof without stats.
- Headline direction: "Built by an indie founder who ships his own subscription app. Same paywall, same checks."
- When to use: About page, Tayo's posts, launch service. **Never** add revenue, download or rating numbers unless Tayo supplies and verifies them.

**Angle 4: One distinct app beats twenty clones (contrarian)**
- The angle: the "pump out apps" playbook is now how you lose a developer account. Ship fewer apps that are clearly yours.
- Why it works: it goes against Vibecode-style "$45K a month" dreams. It matches the 3%-of-revenue data for new apps and r/vibecoding's "nobody uses your vibecoded apps" (519 upvotes).
- Headline direction: "Stop shipping clones. Ship the app only you would make."
- When to use: thought-leadership posts and app-factory founders. Risk: it can read as preachy. Pair it with the tool.

**Angle 5: Paywall that passes review (specificity)**
- The angle: the paywall comes built in, with price, period, restore and terms, the things 3.1.2 rejections cite.
- Why it works: paywall-missing-terms is a named rejection cause. "revenuecat" searches are up 147%.
- Headline direction: "A subscription paywall with the parts Apple checks, already in."
- When to use: paywall SEO cluster (cluster C). A supporting feature page, not the lead. Superapp also claims day-one subscriptions.

## Recommended starting point

**Lead angle: Angle 1, "Check before Apple does."** It is the only angle that expresses the differentiator in [differentiators.md](differentiators.md). It also answers the top complaint.

**Sequence, because Angle 1 is not true yet:**
1. **Now to launch of pre-flight:** run Angle 2 (free kit) as the public message, with Angle 3 as the voice. Collect a waitlist for "pre-flight" on the kit's download page.
2. **When pre-flight + 4.3 check ship:** switch the homepage hero to Angle 1.
3. Keep Angle 4 for Tayo's posts. Keep Angle 5 for the paywall pages.
