## Step 1 — What we actually sell (the transformation)
Not "AI video". After: a new listing goes live and, the same day, the agent has a tour reel, a hook reel and a set of Meta ads that look like the listing ads that already work in their town. Nothing in them is invented. They paid for that one listing, not for a year. Pain that disappears: the $300–750 videographer invoice, the CapCut evening, the "will this AI edit get me fined" worry, and the annual charge in a month with no listings.

## Step 2 — Alternatives and their weak spots
| Alternative | Weak spot (evidence) |
|---|---|
| Do nothing / post the photo gallery | "A slow room-by-room video with music is often just a worse version of the photo gallery" (Reddit, SaaS deep-dive) — agents half-agree video is optional |
| DIY (CapCut, Canva, brokerage templates) | Free, but "none of it looks professionally done"; time cost; same templates as every agent |
| Hire a videographer | $300–750 a listing (VideoTour's own LP math; "$450 every listing" Reel-E review) |
| Photo-to-video subscription app | Annual-default pricing, credits, no refunds; AI that adds doors and fridges; generic look |
| Social autopilot subscription | $119/mo whether you list or not; generic content churns ("boring and basic") |
| Managed ad service | $1,000–2,000/mo; "got zip out of it" |

## Step 3 — Unique mechanism
**Modeled on local winners.** We read the public Meta Ad Library for the agent's metro, keep only listing ads that other agents have paid to run 30+ days, and rebuild that structure (hook, shot order, caption style) with the agent's own photos. Run length is a proxy for "this paid its way", not proof of sales — we say so.
Secondary mechanism: **motion, not generation** — the camera moves over the real photo; no new pixels.

## Step 4 — Market sophistication: Stage 3 moving into 4
- Stage 3 evidence: 8+ SaaS tools at $19–99 making the same "photos → video in minutes" claim; brokerages give it away; VideoTour's own ads have shifted to "you're doing it wrong" lists (28 copies) and named mechanisms ("Cinematic Spatial Reconstruction™").
- Stage 4 evidence: skepticism and ridicule of AI listing video on r/realtors ("catfishing the listing", the extra-fridge thread), "Animoto wants its c. 2010 idea back", agents doubting video sells homes at all.
- Implication: plain superiority claims ("fastest", "best") are dead. Lead with a **mechanism** (Stage 3) and wrap it in an **identity** the jaded buyer wants to belong to (Stage 4): the agent who markets accurately and does what's proven locally.

## Angle options

**Angle 1: Local Proof (unique mechanism)**
- The angle: Your listing ads, modeled on the ones agents in your market have kept paying to run, rebuilt with your photos.
- Why it works: Stage 3 buyers need a "why this works when others don't". Copying what survives locally is believable, and it answers "generic content" (gap #4) without us claiming results we can't prove.
- Headline direction: "Your next listing ad, modeled on the ones that lasted in your market."
- When to use: Ads to agents already running or considering Meta ads; teams; landing hero. This is the differentiator in plain words.

**Angle 2: Nothing Invented (enemy: AI that guesses)**
- The angle: Every frame comes from your real photos. The original sits next to each clip. If something appears that isn't in your photos, we redo it or refund it.
- Why it works: Allays the loudest fear about AI video (7 Reddit threads, ~250 comments, MLS fines of $2,500 cited). Confirms a suspicion agents already hold. Blair Warren: "confirm their suspicions".
- Headline direction: "Your seller's house, not an AI's guess."
- When to use: Agents in CA (AB 723) and strict MLS markets; retargeting people who watched a demo but didn't upload; anyone burned by an AI edit. Note: VideoTour also *claims* "AI never touches the room" — this angle only wins if we show proof (side-by-side), not if we just say it.

**Angle 3: Pay When You List (risk reversal + constraint refused)**
- The angle: $49 a listing. No subscription, no annual plan, no credits. 30-day money-back on your first payment.
- Why it works: Billing traps are the #1 category complaint (71 of 623 low-star reviews, 9 products). Listing demand is seasonal (Nov–Feb slow); a subscription charges in empty months.
- Headline direction: "$49 a listing. Nothing in the months you don't list."
- When to use: Solo listing agents; around competitor discount pushes (Black Friday from ~Nov 24); price-in-ad tests (REC's structure).

**Angle 4: Win the Listing (transformation, seller-facing)**
- The angle: Walk into the listing appointment with the seller's ad plan already made — built from what works in their neighborhood's market.
- Why it works: Agents are paid on listings, not videos. VideoTour tested this only as long copy and killed it (~27 days); a short, demo-led version is untested open ground.
- Headline direction: "Show the seller their ad plan before they sign."
- When to use: Spring prep (Jan–Mar, when sellers decide); listing-presentation content; teams.

**Angle 5: The Accurate Agent (identity, Stage 4)**
- The angle: For agents who'd rather be accurate than flashy.
- Why it works: Jaded markets buy belonging. It excludes the lifestyle-scene crowd on purpose and flatters a self-image many agents already hold (licensed, careful, liable).
- Headline direction: "Listing marketing for agents who'd rather be right than flashy."
- When to use: Brand line, founder letter, email signature; not strong enough alone as an ad hook.

## Recommended starting point
**Angle 1 (Local Proof) as the lead, with Angle 2's proof (originals side by side) shown in the creative and Angle 3's price in the offer line.** Reason: it is the only angle built on the `OURS` differentiator, it fits a Stage 3→4 market (mechanism + identity), and it feeds the lead magnet directly. Angle 2 is the first challenger in the ad test (see [metrics.md](metrics.md) T1), because its emotional pull may be stronger even though it is less differentiated.
