## Daisy chain (one idea, five assets)
**Core idea:** *Your market already shows you which listing ads work — the ones agents keep paying to run. Start there, with your real photos.*

### 1. Problem-unaware post (full copy)
**Trailer:**
Line 1: Scroll your town's listing reels and count how many open on the same pan-left across a living room.
Line 2: It's not because that shot works. It's because it's the default.

**Meat:**
Most listing videos come from the same five or six templates. Brokerage tools, the big apps, the Canva packs. Same transitions, same music, same "Just Listed" title card in the same font.

Here's a quick test you can do in two minutes:
1. Open Instagram or Facebook and search your city plus "just listed".
2. Watch the first three seconds of ten listing videos with the sound off.
3. Write down what each one opens on.

When we've done this, most of the ten open the same way. A buyer scrolling past sees one listing, ten times.

The fix isn't fancier effects. It's starting from something other than the default.

**Summary:** If your listing video opens like everyone else's, the buyer's thumb treats it like everyone else's.

**CTC:** Do the ten-video test in your city. What did most of them open on?

*Testable expectation:* comments that name an opening shot (proves the test was done). Problem-unaware posts are judged on comments and saves, not clicks. Kill the format if <5 substantive comments across 3 posts.

### 2. Problem-aware post (full copy)
**Trailer:**
Line 1: The best guide to listing ads in your city is free, public, and almost no agent uses it.
Line 2: No, it's not a course.

**Meat:**
Meta's Ad Library shows every ad running on Facebook and Instagram, including the start date. An ad that's been running for 30, 90, 300 days is usually an ad someone keeps paying for. Ads that don't work get switched off fast.

How to use it for your next listing:
1. Go to the Meta Ad Library, set the country to the US and the category to "All ads".
2. Search your city plus "just listed" or "open house".
3. Open each result and check "Started running on". Ignore anything under 30 days.
4. For the ones that lasted, note three things: the first shot, where the price appears, and the call to action.
5. Build your next listing's ad on that structure. Your own photos. Your own words.

Two rules: copy the structure, never another agent's photos or footage. And describe the home, not who should live in it — that's fair-housing basics.

**Summary:** Ads that lasted in your market are the closest thing to a free answer key. Use the structure, not the content.

**CTC:** What's the longest-running listing ad you can find in your city? Drop the number of days.

*Testable expectation:* replies with day counts; link clicks to the supporting article. Healthy: 1–3% CTR on the article link (UNSOURCED).
