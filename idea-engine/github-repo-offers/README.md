# Is there demand? 29 offers from Corey Ganim's "GitHub repos that print money" videos

Checked 2026-11-10 with the Gate 0 method in [METHOD.md](METHOD.md). That means:
- **Buyers asking:** 20+ requests from buyers in the last 30–60 days.
- **Competitors:** 3–5 of them, with prices read from their live pricing pages.
- **Margin:** at least 50% gross margin with honest hours.
- **Watering holes:** named places where the buyers can be found and reached.

Each offer has its own scorecard with links.

## The answer

**No offer passes as pitched.** 0 of 29 clears all four checks. 25 are rejected. 4 show real money nearby and are worth parking, but each only works narrower or priced differently than in the video.

Three patterns sink almost every offer:

1. **The buyer already owns the tool.**
   - QuickBooks sends payment reminders and turns forwarded invoices into bills.
   - NotebookLM makes the podcasts for free.
   - YouTube dubs videos for free.
   - HubSpot's built-in AI preps calls.
   - ChatGPT and Copilot seats answer staff questions from company documents.
   - UptimeRobot monitors 50 sites for free.

   A $250–500/mo retainer can't sit on top of a $0–49 tool.
2. **Sellers outnumber buyers.** For AI receptionists, buying-intent alerts, local SEO audits, clipping and SOP chatbots, sellers beat buyers 4–10 to 1. Many of the "sellers" are other students of the same courses.
3. **His hours are fiction.** "3 hours a month" for 40 curated clips is about 5 minutes a clip. Daily replies written in the client's voice take about 8 hours a month. With real hours most margins fall to 20–40%, and some go negative.

His lead source ("scrape job posts for the role this replaces") often points at the wrong buyer:
- **Estimators** price jobs; they don't find bids.
- **File clerks** are hired by hospitals and prisons.
- **"Proposal writer"** jobs are architecture and engineering tender teams working in InDesign.
- **AP clerks and reporting analysts** are mostly hired by enterprises.

The repos themselves are real. A few don't do what he says:
- **OpenManus** has no inbox or chat-app integration.
- **Text-to-CAD** takes text only, not photos.
- **Impeccable** polishes a site's design; it doesn't build or migrate one.
- **Postiz** schedules posts; it doesn't write them.
- **OpenSEO** has no Google Business Profile or map-pack tracking.

Two licences block the offer outright:
- **Invoice Ninja** (Elastic License) forbids hosting it as a service for third parties, which is exactly the offer.
- **OpenShorts'** cloud module forbids paid hosting.

## Limits of this check

- **Upwork and Fiverr:** both block direct search, so their counts are samples from Google's index, and many counts are lower bounds.
- **Hours:** the hours in each margin are estimates.
- **Unverified claims:**
  - Corey's own sales: the $7,000 barber-school GPT and the $3,000 listing pack.
  - A few vendor prices that came from third-party sites. These are marked in each file.
- **What a real test would be:** the next step for a 🟡 offer is 20 targeted conversations or a paid pilot, not more desk research.
