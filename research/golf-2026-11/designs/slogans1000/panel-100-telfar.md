# Telfar-adjacent shopper panel: golf rope cap slogans

**SIMULATED, not demand evidence.** 100 simulated shoppers whose reactions were written by hand from the wording of each slogan. No scoring script was used. Use this panel to generate hypotheses, then test them with real people.

Product: ivory retro rope cap, slogan small and centred in forest green, from a new, unknown golf brand (not Telfar), $34, mid-November.

Panel: 100 shoppers (25 Telfar core, 20 golfcore fashion, 15 culture golfers, 15 fit-check posters, 15 fast-fashion deal buyers, 10 gift buyers); 70 under 35; 55 women, 40 men, 5 nonbinary. Each shopper saw a balanced random 15 of the 35 slogans in random order, so each slogan was shown 42-43 times. All rates are per exposure (choices / times shown).

Overall: 28/100 would buy their first choice now, 58/100 would post or share it, and 82/100 would wear their pick off the course.

## Two most common reasons: top 5 and bottom 5

### Top 5

- **T32 Birdies in My Bag**: 1) Bag double meaning: reads as a wink to bag culture as well as a golf bag (about 9 of 11 first-choosers said this); 2) Works as a fit-pic or flat-lay piece next to a handbag (3); the brake on buying was the unknown brand, not the line.
- **T28 Respectfully, That's OB**: 1) Genuinely funny, polite clap-back golf humour that won't date (most first-choosers); 2) But 6 of 9 first-choosers would only wear it on the course or would buy it as a gift: the joke needs you to golf.
- **T22 Bogey. What About It?**: 1) Unbothered attitude ('what about it?') that suits a fit pic and how people talk about their game (7 of 8); 2) Doesn't borrow anyone's slang, so it doesn't feel like a brand trying to get into the group chat (4).
- **T19 Birdie Behavior**: 1) Short, clean, looks designed rather than memed; easy to wear off the course (all 5); 2) Seen as safe, so buy intent was zero: people liked it but wanted to see the cap and the brand first (3).
- **T03 Mind Your Mulligans**: 1) Clever golf wordplay that is 'grown' and doesn't try to talk like the shopper (all 5); 2) Fits a vintage, country-club rope-cap look; older and golfing shoppers liked it more.

### Bottom 5

- **T10 It's Giving Birdie**: 1) 'It's giving' is played out and overused (9 most-disliked); 2) Rated trying too hard by 91% of those who saw it; the one fan liked it as a deliberately silly fit-check prop.
- **T33 Driver Bussin'**: 1) Cringe, a dad joke; 'nobody talks like that about a driver' (24 most-disliked, 56% of exposures); 2) Trying too hard for 95% of those who saw it; the one fan wanted it ironically.
- **T29 Birdie Aura**: 1) Inoffensive but forgettable: never most-disliked, never trying too hard, picked only as a backup (5 second choices); 2) Nobody's first choice because 'aura' alone gives no joke and no golf hook.
- **T14 Main Birdie Energy**: 1) A weaker copy of 'Main Tee Energy' (54% trying too hard); 2) No first or second choices; adds 'birdie' to a meme without adding a joke.
- **T24 Go, Birdie Bestie**: 1) Childish, corny, 'a kids' party banner' (9 most-disliked, 21% of exposures); 2) Trying too hard for 60%; even gift buyers chose the plainer 'Birdie Bestie' instead.

## Conclusion

The strongest lines were the ones that don't borrow slang and either have a double meaning this audience already owns (Birdies in My Bag) or an attitude that holds up off the course (Bogey. What About It?, Birdie Behavior, Mind Your Mulligans). Heavy slang (Driver Bussin', It's Giving Birdie, Birdie. Periodt., Delulu Birdie, the 'era' lines) and 'Putt for the Culture' were most often called trying too hard. Shoppers read them as an unknown golf brand borrowing language and expected them to date quickly, although 4 shoppers chose 'Putt for the Culture' (2 culture golfers, a Telfar core fan and a gift buyer), and only if the brand showed up in the community. The golf-insider jokes (Respectfully, That's OB; Slice in 4K) landed with golfers and gift buyers but mostly stayed on the course, and buy intent was limited by the unknown brand more than by the slogan.
