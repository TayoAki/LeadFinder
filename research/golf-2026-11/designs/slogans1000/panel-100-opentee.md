# Open Tee Club: simulated shopper panel (100)

> **SIMULATED, not demand evidence.** These 100 shoppers are hand-written simulations. Each reaction was written from the words of the slogan or name, and scripts only randomised the exposures and counted the answers. Use the results to decide what to put in front of real buyers, not to estimate demand.

Product: an Ivory retro rope cap with small forest-green embroidery, $34 for a slogan cap and $38 for a personalised crew cap, no minimums. The brand is new and unknown and runs community meetups. Panel date: mid-November 2026.

**Panel:** 22 new women golfers, 18 off-course golf-curious, 15 culture golfers, 15 crew organisers, 12 fashion-first, 6 women's group or league leaders and 12 gift buyers. That is 63 women, 35 men and 2 non-binary shoppers; 62 are under 35.

**Design:** each shopper saw a balanced random 12 of the slogans in random order, so every slogan was shown 48 times. Each saw all 10 crew names in random order and B1-B3 in random order. All rates below are per exposure.

**Note:** the brief said 27 slogans, but `panel-shortlist-opentee.json` contains 25 (S01-S25). All 25 were tested.

**Reactions to "Open Tee Club":** welcoming 58, generic 37, confusing 3, exclusive 2.

Most common reactions, as the shoppers put them:

- **Welcoming (58):** "'Open' sounds like I could just turn up to a meetup without knowing anyone." "Feels like a club you're invited into, not one you apply to." Many added the condition *if the meetups are real*.
- **Generic (37):** "Fine, forgettable." "Sounds like a tee-time booking app." "There are a hundred 'something Club' brands." Fashion-first shoppers said "Public Tee Club" has more of a point of view, and "Everybody" says it louder.
- **Confusing (3):** some heard it as British Open / Open Championship merchandise.
- **Exclusive (2):** "'Club' makes me think there are dues." "Sounds like a private club with 'Open' stuck on the front."

## Why: the 2 most common reasons

**Top 5 slogans** (reasons for picking it first or second):

- **S19 Bogey. What About It?:** owns bad golf with confidence / self-roast (30)
- **S21 Respectfully, That's OB:** golf-literate wit (21); safe, positive gift message (1)
- **S25 Here for the Birdies:** simple, upbeat, beginner-safe (7); safe, positive gift message (4)
- **S24 Slice in 4K:** owns bad golf with confidence / self-roast (17)
- **S09 Tough Bounce:** understated real-golf phrase (15)

**Bottom 5 slogans** (reasons for making it the most disliked, plus how many flagged it as trying too hard):

- **S08 Big Tee Energy:** crude double meaning, not wearable around family/work (13). Flagged as trying too hard by 1 of 48.
- **S16 Range Crashout:** negative/meltdown message (2). Flagged as trying too hard by 36 of 48.
- **S03 Eagle Girl Era:** eagles are unrealistic for beginners (6); not for me (gendered) (1). Flagged as trying too hard by 8 of 48.
- **S10 It's Giving Birdie:** already-dated meme phrase (10). Flagged as trying too hard by 45 of 48.
- **S07 Driver Bussin':** borrowed slang from an unknown brand (cringe) (21). Flagged as trying too hard by 47 of 48.

Where only one reason is listed, every pick or dislike of that slogan gave the same reason.


Representative quotes: S19 "It says you don't have to be good to show up." S21 "The only one that sounds like a person wrote it." S07 "Brands borrowing slang they don't speak; hard pass." S10 "'It's Giving' already sounds like 2022." S08 "I'm not wearing a tee joke to a work golf event."

## Conclusion

In this simulated panel, plain golf attitude wins again: "Bogey. What About It?" was picked first by 35% of the shoppers who saw it, made the top two for 63%, drew zero dislikes, and won in four of the seven segments (new women golfers, culture golfers, crew organisers and fashion-first). Wry, golf-literate lines ("Respectfully, That's OB", "Tough Bounce") and owning bad shots ("Slice in 4K", "Shank in 4K") make up the next tier. Borrowed internet slang ("Driver Bussin'", "It's Giving Birdie", "No Cap, All Carry", "Birdie Aura", the "Crashout" lines) was flagged as trying too hard by 54-98% of the shoppers who saw it, and lines with "girl" in them split shoppers: some said "that's me", while others called them infantilising or said they left them out.

Crew caps are the stronger product: 48% would buy one for their own group (13 of 15 crew organisers and all 6 league leaders), compared with 26% who would buy a slogan cap now. "Range Day Girls" and "Tee Time Squad" fit the most groups, while "Slice Boys" and "Back Nine Brunch Club" drew the most dislikes.

"Open Tee Club" was the favourite name (46 votes) and mostly read as welcoming (58), but more than a third called it generic. A few heard the British Open or a club with dues. Only 32% said the brand feels made for people like them. Most were unsure, and they tied their answer to whether the meetups turn out to be real.

These are simulated reactions. They are not demand evidence and should be checked with real pre-orders and meetup sign-ups.
