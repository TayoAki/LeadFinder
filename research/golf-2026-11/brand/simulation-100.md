# Dogleg Club: 100-visitor buyer panel

> **SIMULATED. This is not demand evidence.** The 100 visitors below are judgement calls made by a model playing skeptical buyers. Nobody real visited the site. Use this to rank fixes, never to forecast sales. The visitor-level data is in `simulation-100.json`.

**Setup:** the one-page site from sections 2–4 of `dogleg-club.md`, in mid-November, at launch. It has no reviews, the hero is a labelled mock-up and the brand name is unknown. Prices are $34 Classic, $38 Crew Cap, and $140/$264/$384 packs. US shipping is $4.69 for the first hat and $2.09 for each extra.

## Revenue

- **Orders:** 5.
- **Revenue:** $357.81 including shipping, of which $326 is product.
- **Average order value:** $71.56.
- **Hats sold:** 9, which is **1.8 hats per order**.
- **Orders:**
  - 1 Clubhouse Classic: $38.69.
  - 3 Crew Cap orders:
    - 1 hat as a gift: $42.69;
    - 1 hat as a sample before an 8-man order: $42.69;
    - 2 hats for a dad and his teen: $82.78.
  - 1 Crew Pack of 4: $150.96.

**Which product won:** the **Crew Cap at $38**, in 3 of 5 orders. It is the reason people buy. The packs only sell once the group has already agreed, as with the 4-man trip whose organiser had collected Venmo. The 8-man and 12-man buyers stalled on coordination. One of them bought a single cap as a sample instead. The Classic won one impulse buyer.

## Top reasons people bought

1. **No minimum.** They get the crew name, trip and year on exactly the number of hats they need.
2. **A personalised gift feels thoughtful.** The recipient's own crew name beats a generic golf gift.
3. **The dad and kid matching set** ("CREW: DAD" and "CREW: CADDIE").
4. **A single sample cap is a low-risk test** before committing the group.
5. **A funny Classic line at an impulse price** ($34).

## 5 fixes that would move the most visitors

These estimates are simulated: they count the visitors in this panel who would move from save or no to buy within about 2 weeks.

1. **Real photos and video of stitched samples.**
   - What it is: the hero, rope and stitch close-ups, and the preview shown next to the finished cap.
   - Addresses: the mock-up (18), preview trust (13) and polyester doubts (7).
   - Estimate: **+3 to 4 buyers.** Two trip or captain savers would buy, plus one or two cold visitors. The Pinehurst organiser would likely buy the 8-pack instead of a sample.
2. **Crew order link.**
   - What it is: the organiser picks the design and shares a link. Each member then picks a colour and pays for their own hat.
   - Addresses: group coordination (15).
   - Estimate: **+3 buyers**, mostly trip organisers and captains who saved. It also lifts hats per order, which is where the money is.
3. **A real holiday cut-off date and a digital gift card ("they design their own").**
   - Addresses: holiday timing (11) and gift taste (8).
   - Estimate: **+3 buyers**, mostly gift buyers and Meta clickers who want it as their own gift.
4. **Seeded proof.**
   - What it is: send 10–20 caps to real crews and small creators, then show "Photos from crews" and the first reviews. Email everyone who saved.
   - Addresses: no reviews (29) and no brand cachet (7).
   - Estimate: **+2 to 3 buyers.** It mostly converts the 22 savers on their return visit rather than cold visitors.
5. **Free shipping over $75 and a 2-hat price.**
   - What it is: free shipping over $75 is already an option in the plan; add a price for 2 hats.
   - Addresses: price vs Amazon (14) and shipping on top (4).
   - Estimate: **+1 to 2 buyers.** It also pushes single buyers to 2 hats.

Runner-up fix: a confirmed youth cap for dads would address 6 visitors and gain about 1 buyer.

No fix moves the "just browsing", style-led and non-golfer visitors (about 25). Those visitors are the reason cold slideshow traffic stays near 0–2% whatever the page says.
