# Siamese Mahjong: How to Play 2-Player American Mahjong

Yes, you can play American mahjong with two people. The two-player game is called **Siamese mahjong**:
- Each of you gets **two racks** and builds **two hands at once**.
- There's **no Charleston**.
- The first person to **mahj both hands** wins.

That's the whole idea. The rest of this guide covers setup, how a turn works, scoring, and a few strategy notes. A printable rules sheet is at the end.

*Pip says: two players is a game, not a waiting room. Let's deal.*

## What is Siamese mahjong?

Siamese mahjong (officially *Siamese Mah Jongg®*) is a two-player version of American mahjong. It uses the same 152 tiles and the same card you already play with.

The twist is that each player plays **two hands side by side**, one on each rack. You share one pool of tiles but have twice the room to build, so the game feels full even with two people.

It has caught on quickly. Searches for "siamese mahjong rules" have more than tripled in the past year. It's also how a lot of couples, roommates and parent-and-kid pairs learn to play together.

## What you need

- **A standard American mahjong set:** 152 tiles, including 8 jokers and 8 flowers.
- **Four racks,** two per player. Some companies sell a "double Siamese rack" that holds two hands in one; nice to have, not required.
- **A card for each player,** the National Mah Jongg League card you normally use. The official rules also allow a separate Siamese card, sold by the rule's publisher.
- **One die** if you're playing with walls.

## How to set up Siamese mahjong

There are two common ways to set up. The **official rules** use walls, like a four-player game. A lot of home players use a **quick pool** instead. Both work. Pick one and agree before you start, the same way you'd agree on any table rule.

### The official setup (with walls)

These steps follow the current Siamese Mah Jongg rules published at mahjongg.org:

1. **Racks:** face each other and set two racks in front of each player.
2. **Walls:** shuffle the tiles face down. Each player builds **two walls, 19 stacks long and 2 tiles high,** one in front of each rack. When your first wall runs out, push out the second.
3. **Break the wall:** East throws a **single die** and counts that many stacks from the right of their first wall. Those stacks move to the end of the wall. Both of East's walls are used before the opponent's.
4. **Deal:** each player takes **four tiles at a time** (two stacks) from East's wall until **both players have 27 tiles.**
5. **Start:** East picks a **28th tile** from the wall and discards. Play begins.

One rule changed recently. Older versions gave East 28 tiles in the deal and the other player 27. The rules were revised so both players get 27, to remove East's small statistical edge. If you learned it the old way, that's why your teacher's handout may differ.

### The quick home setup (no walls)

Many teachers use this for casual play, and it's how Southern Sparrow teaches "date night mahjong":

1. Two racks each.
2. Shuffle all the tiles **face down in the middle** of the table.
3. Each player **draws their own tiles** from the pool: the dealer takes **28**, the other player **27**.
4. The dealer discards first.

The official rules don't support playing without walls in tournaments, since walls stop anyone "remembering" where the jokers went. For a relaxed game at home, the pool is faster. If you play this way, stir the pool now and then.

## How to play Siamese mahjong, step by step

Once the tiles are dealt, a turn works just like a four-player game:

1. **Draw or call.** Take a tile from the wall (or pool), or call your opponent's discard if you can use it for an exposure.
2. **Arrange.** Move tiles **freely between your two racks.** This is the fun part: a joker can go wherever it helps most.
3. **Discard** one tile face up.

### Do you do the Charleston in Siamese mahjong?

No. **No Charleston and no courtesy pass.** You're working with roughly half the tiles in play across your two racks, so you already have plenty of options. Skipping the Charleston is also why Siamese is so quick to start.

### Tiles in your racks vs. on your racks

You can shuffle tiles between racks as much as you like, but **exposures stay put.** Once you expose tiles on a rack, they belong to that hand. If the exposures on a rack don't match any hand on the card, that hand is dead.

The official way to say it: tiles are interchangeable *in* the racks, not *on* the racks.

### Jokers

You can swap for a joker in **your own** exposures or **your opponent's.** You can't take a joker from a hand that's already been declared mahjong.

### How do you win?

**The first player to declare mahjong on both hands wins.** The game also ends if the wall runs out with no mahjong at all, which is a wall game.

If one of your hands goes dead, you can keep playing the other. You just can't win the game with two mahjongs anymore.

## Scoring Siamese mahjong

If you play for points, the official rules score like this:
- **Your first mahjong** scores its value on the card. Jokerless hands double, except hands made only of singles and pairs.
- **Your second mahjong** (the one that wins) scores **double.** Jokerless doubles again.
- **Declaring both mahjongs at the same time** doubles the score for **both** hands.
- **Throwing the tile for your opponent's second mahjong** costs you **25 points.**
- **Wall game:** each player gets **10 points.**

**Easy payouts:** keep a running score and settle the difference at the end of the session.

## What are the strategies for playing Siamese mahjong?

A few things that make Siamese feel different from the four-player game:

- **One sure hand, one flexible hand.** It's normal to feel confident about one rack and fuzzy about the other. Push the strong hand toward mahjong and keep the second loose until the tiles tell you where to go.
- **Move jokers to where they're needed.** With two racks, a joker can switch hands as late as the turn you need it.
- **Think about declaring your first mahjong early.** Two reasons, both from experienced teachers:
  1. It locks that hand, so your opponent can't swap out your exposed jokers.
  2. You can only call a discard for a single or pair when it's your final tile for mahjong. In Siamese you have two "final tiles" to aim for, so declaring one hand can get you a tile you couldn't otherwise call.
- **Watch the second-mahjong discard.** Late in the game, be careful what you throw. Feeding your opponent's second mahjong costs 25 points on top of their doubled score.

## What is the easiest hand to win in mahjong?

The card changes every year, and we don't reprint it here. But the general rule holds: **hands built from pungs, kongs and quints are easier**, because jokers can stand in for those tiles. **Hands made of singles and pairs are harder**, because jokers can't be used in them at all.

In Siamese you're building two hands, so leaning on joker-friendly sections of the card for at least one rack is a sensible start.

## Can you play mahjong with three players?

Yes. The same rule-makers publish **Royale Siamese Mah Jongg**, which applies the two-racks-each idea to three or four players. Plain three-player American mahjong (one rack each, a smaller Charleston) is another option. We'll cover both in our 3-player guide.

## Is it fun to play mahjong with two players?

Honestly, it might be the best way to practice.
- **Setup is fast** and there's no Charleston, so you're playing within minutes.
- **You build two hands every game**, which is roughly twice the practice of a four-player game.
- **It's great for teaching.** Several teachers say Siamese is the easiest way to get a partner hooked, because you skip straight to the playing part.

And when the new card comes out each spring, a few rounds of Siamese is the quickest way to learn the new hands.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can you play American mahjong with just two people?
Yes. The two-player version is called Siamese mahjong. Each player uses two racks and builds two hands at once, and the first player to complete both hands wins.

### What is 2-player mahjong called?
Siamese mahjong (Siamese Mah Jongg®). The version for three or four players, with two racks per player, is called Royale Siamese Mah Jongg.

### How many tiles do you deal for Siamese mahjong?
Under the current official rules, 27 tiles each, taken four at a time from East's wall. East then picks a 28th tile to start. Many casual players simply draw 28 for the dealer and 27 for the other player.

### How many tiles are in an American mahjong set?
152: four each of the numbered tiles in three suits (dots, bams, craks), 16 winds, 12 dragons, 8 flowers and 8 jokers.

### How to play 2 person mahjong for beginners?
Use the quick home setup: two racks each, tiles face down in the middle, dealer draws 28 and the other player 27, no Charleston. Then play normal turns and try to complete both hands.

### Do you do the Charleston with Siamese mahjong?
No. There's no Charleston and no courtesy pass.

### What are the strategies for playing Siamese mahjong?
Commit to one strong hand and keep the other flexible. Move jokers to whichever hand needs them. Consider declaring your first mahjong early to protect your exposed jokers.

### Why do so many Jewish women play mahjong?
American mahjong grew up in Jewish American communities. The National Mah Jongg League was founded in New York in 1937, and the game became a fixture of social life for generations of Jewish American women. Many of today's new players learned from a mother or grandmother.
