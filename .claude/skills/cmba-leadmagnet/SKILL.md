# CMBA — Lead Magnet, Landing Page, Survey

Three linked artifacts. The transcript's flywheel runs *backwards* from the usual advice: you do
not need an offer first. The magnet gets the email, the **survey tells you what to build**, and
the offer then informs future magnets.

## Process

### 1. The narrow problem
The magnet solves a **narrow** problem whose solution *exposes* a wider, more expensive problem —
which is exactly what the core offer solves.

Write both explicitly:
- Narrow problem: [small, tactical, completable in one sitting]
- Wide problem it exposes: [expensive, ongoing, what the offer solves]
- The bridge sentence: *"Great, you've [narrow win]. But what are you doing about [wide problem]?"*

Failure test: if solving the narrow problem leaves nothing to sell, it's too generous. If solving
it doesn't naturally raise the wide problem, the funnel has no pull.

### 2. Format via the quadrant
Axes: easy↔hard to consume, low↔high engagement. **Never build from the bottom half.**

| | Easy to consume | Hard to consume |
|---|---|---|
| **High engagement** | templates, 10–15 min on-demand video, short live tutorial, toolkit → *simple/affordable offers* | 1:1 consult, long training, 60-min webinar, multi-page worksheet → *complex/expensive offers* |
| **Low engagement** | infographic, resource guide, cheat sheet → *never* | white paper, case study, ebook → *never* |

Pick the quadrant by matching offer complexity and price. Top-right yields fewer but far hotter
leads; top-left yields volume. State the choice and why.

**The recurring-publication case (not in the original quadrant).**
The quadrant plots *one-off downloadables*. A newsletter, podcast or channel is a different
instrument and the narrow-problem rule does not apply to it — it solves a wide problem, forever,
free, and works by **compounding trust** rather than by opening a gap.

If the subject's primary magnet is recurring:
- Do not try to force it into a quadrant cell, and do not "fix" it for violating the narrow rule
- Grade it on retention and reply rate, not on the narrow/wide gap
- The one-shot magnet's job changes: it is no longer the top of the funnel, it is a **feeder** into
  the recurring one. Design it to convert a stranger into a subscriber, not into a buyer.
- The narrow/wide gap still governs the one-shot feeder

Observed on Why We Buy: a 63,353-subscriber newsletter is the highest-performing magnet in the test
set and breaks the framework's central rule. The rule is about one-shot assets.

### 3. Landing page — four required elements
1. **Attention-grabbing headline** — combine the two things the audience most wants. Model:
   *"Start your [journey] with [number] [outcome-generating] [assets]"*
2. **Image that makes it tangible** — a box/mockup even for a digital asset. Tested to lift
   conversion materially
3. **Transformation-rich copy** — three benefit lines, transformation not features
   ("unlock… seize… supercharge…", not "download the PDF")
4. **Minimal form** — email only by default; first name only if personalization is actually used

Plus, from the copy lesson: fifth/sixth-grade reading level, clarity over cleverness, one primary
CTA, and **trust builders** (testimonials with faces + stars, verifiable stats, logos) — only real
ones. If none exist, say so and design the page to work without them.

### 4. Post-download survey (the highest-leverage step)
Never a plain thank-you page. Redirect to a survey at peak engagement.

Three multiple-choice questions minimum, one open text last:
1. **Where are you on the journey?** (not started / side income / full-time)
2. **Biggest challenge right now?** — 4–5 pre-written options that prove you understand them + Other
3. **What have you tried / what format do you prefer?** (course / coaching / service / cohort)
4. **Open text**: one question in their own words — this is where the next product comes from

Tooling by budget: Google Forms (free) → Typeform (logic) → RightMessage (personalizes downstream
email). Recommend one based on their actual stage.

## Evaluation

**Rubric (0–4 each, 20 total). Pass ≥15, no dimension <2.**

| # | Dimension | 0 | 2 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Faithfulness** | Generic freebie idea | Magnet + page | Narrow/wide pair explicit, quadrant justified, 4 page elements, survey with 3 MC + 1 open |
| 2 | **Narrow/wide gap** | Magnet solves everything or nothing | Gap exists but weak | Solving the narrow problem provably surfaces the wide one; bridge sentence is persuasive |
| 3 | **Page conversion logic** | Feature copy, long form | Some transformation copy | Headline fuses two known desires, transformation copy in verbs, form is 1 field, single CTA |
| 4 | **Survey leverage** | Demographic trivia | Reasonable questions | Every question maps to a stated downstream decision; MC options read like the audience's own words |
| 5 | **Falsifiability** | No target | "Track conversions" | States expected conversion rate band and what result would mean headline vs. offer vs. traffic is the problem |

**Kill check:** a magnet from the bottom half of the quadrant (cheat sheet, infographic, ebook,
white paper) presented as the recommendation, or fabricated testimonials/social proof on the page.
Either zeroes the run.

**Control comparison:** if the company has a live opt-in, diff against it — element by element
against the four required. Count which of the four they're missing; that count is the concrete
finding, not the prose.

## Failure modes seen in testing

- "Ultimate guide" ebooks — bottom-right quadrant, the exact thing the lesson warns against
- Survey questions the operator finds interesting rather than ones that decide a product
- Landing page with 3 CTAs and a nav bar (distraction kills the single conversion action)
- Choosing top-left volume format for a $5k service, or top-right webinar for a $27 product
