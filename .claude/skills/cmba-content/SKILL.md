# CMBA — Content Engine

The transcript's non-negotiable: **teach**. No platitudes, no pet photos, no humour-for-humour.
Target people who already know they have a problem and are actively looking for a solution —
they are cheaper to reach than the problem-unaware, and they convert.

## Inputs

`uvp.md` (for audience + language) and `idea.md`.

**Required for a real company: read 5–10 pieces of their existing content before writing anything.**
Not optional. Two things depend on it and both fail silently without it:

- **Voice cannot be matched from a description.** Sentence length, person (first vs second),
  formality, and whether they use analogies are only observable by reading.
- **You can misdiagnose a working strategy as an absent one.** Observed on ATX Clean: the first run
  implied there was no content strategy. There were seven posts, five titled as local search
  questions, already publishing cost transparency. The real gaps were narrower and different —
  commercial coverage and service-area pages — and the run only found them after reading.

If no existing content exists, say so explicitly; that is a different and much rarer situation than
not having looked.

## Process

### 0. Distribution mode (choose before building anything)

The teaching principle is universal. The **channel assumptions are not** — daily posting, smart
commenting, and hub-and-spoke into a newsletter are a *creator-business* acquisition model.

| Mode | When | Distribution layer |
|---|---|---|
| **Audience-led** (default) | buyer discovers you by following you | LinkedIn/X daily calendar, smart commenting, newsletter hub, 730-day replay |
| **Intent-led / local** | buyer discovers you by searching at the moment of need — local services, trades, high-intent B2B | service-area + pillar pages, Google Business Profile as the spoke surface, review velocity as the primary growth metric, local trade groups instead of commenting |

In intent-led mode, replace the 90-day daily calendar with **~12 substantial assets over 90 days**,
ranked by intent depth. One service-area page for each named service area beats ninety posts.

Carries over unchanged in both modes: teach-only, the content matrix, trailer/meat/summary + CTC,
smart curation, the hook bank, and seasonal replay.

Observed on ATX Clean Pressure Washing: 204 reviews at 4.9 were doing all the acquisition work, and
there was no email list anywhere on the property. A 90-day LinkedIn calendar would have been
sincere, well-structured, and irrelevant.

### 1. Content matrix
Build the grid, then pair cells into headlines.

**Themes** (8–9 micro-topics that roll up to the umbrella topic)
**Styles**: observation · actionable · teachable step-by-step · motivational · analytical ·
contrarian · X vs Y · present-vs-future prediction · listicle

Pair theme × style → write a **headline only**. Headlines are the unit of ideation; full posts
come later. Produce 15–20 pairings.

### 2. 90-day teachable calendar
13 weeks × 7 days. Every entry is a *teachable* idea — something the reader can do after reading.
Group by 5 sub-topics × 18 ideas. Mark which funnel stage each serves (problem-aware or
solution-aware; save problem-unaware for later).

### 3. Copywriting — trailer / meat / summary
Write the **meat first** (the actual teaching), then the trailer.

- **Trailer** = line 1 (scroll-stopper) + line 2 (intrigue *or* objection handler). On LinkedIn
  these are the only lines before "see more". The entire job is earning that click.
  - Intrigue variant: "…that five-figure business owners don't."
  - Objection-handler variant: "…even if you have less than 5,000 followers."
- **Meat** = the 3–5 teaching points, concrete and specific
- **Summary** = repackage the idea so the reader can comment without scrolling back up
- **CTC (call to conversation)**, not CTA — ask a question that invites participation

Deliver 3–5 fully written posts, not templates.

### 4. Smart curation (sourcing)
Six sources, 2–3 ideas each: AnswerSocrates · AnswerThePublic · YouTube (channel → Videos →
Most popular) · Google autocomplete stems · social comments/DMs on your own and competitors'
content · competitor blogs/newsletters. Record the source next to each idea.

### 5. Hub and spoke
One hub (newsletter / blog / long video / podcast) → 7–10 spokes, each a different style:
story · observation · listicle · contrarian take · past-vs-present · case study · prediction ·
step-by-step. Every spoke links back to the hub. Include the pre-promo (day before) and the
post-promo (day after: "yesterday I wrote…") posts.

### 6. Hook bank
20–30 hooks harvested from proven posts in adjacent niches, rewritten — not copied — for this
audience. Rule: each hook is reusable ~3 times before it burns; over-used hooks actively fail.

### 7. Play the hits
Set up the 730-day library: every post gets a 1–10 score, and winners are auto-scheduled to
repost at +182 / +364 / +546 / +728 days (adjustable to 90-day cadence). Six months of posting
produces two years of calendar.

## Evaluation

**Rubric (0–4 each, 20 total). Pass ≥15, no dimension <2.**

| # | Dimension | 0 | 2 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Faithfulness** | Random content ideas | Matrix or calendar but not both | Matrix → headlines → calendar → written posts → curation → hub/spoke → hooks → replay all present |
| 2 | **Teachability** | Platitudes, hype, "I did X and it changed everything" | Mixed | Every calendar entry is something the reader can *do*; posts contain specific mechanics, not motivation |
| 3 | **Hook quality** | Hooks are labels ("Thoughts on email marketing") | Some tension | Line 1 stops scroll, line 2 adds intrigue or handles the real objection; contrast words present; no hedge words |
| 4 | **Voice match** | Sounds like an AI marketing blog | Approximately right register | Matches the operator's actual sentence length, vocabulary and stance; verifiable against their existing posts |
| 5 | **Falsifiability** | No way to tell what worked | Vague "test it" | Each post carries a testable expectation (which stage, what response, what CTC answer would prove it landed) |

**Kill check:** copied hooks or posts reproduced verbatim from a named creator. Rewriting is the
instruction; plagiarism zeroes the run. Also zero if fabricated stats appear inside post copy.

**Control comparison:** put 3 generated posts beside 3 of the company's real posts. Ask which set
teaches something concrete. If the generated set is more generic, the skill failed regardless of
volume produced.

## Failure modes seen in testing

- Producing 90 titles that are really 12 ideas restated
- Writing the trailer first, which produces hooks with nothing behind them
- CTA ("link in bio") instead of CTC (a question)
- Hooks that are all the same shape ("Here are N things…") — enforce style variety
